# Specification
Translate validated intent and discovery into testable requirements. Cover user behavior, accessibility, authentication, authorization, data, failure behavior, performance, operations, and applicable infrastructure. Requirements must be implementation-independent and traceable. Accessibility requirements follow `.ai/rules/accessibility.md`: at least one `category: accessibility` requirement whenever the change declares `surfaces.accessibility: true`, otherwise a statement of why nothing a person perceives or operates changes. Policy requirements follow `.ai/rules/policy-conformance.md`: at least one `category: policy` requirement whenever the change declares `surfaces.policy: true`, naming what the change collects, shares, retains, or promises differently and which published document and clause covers it, otherwise a statement of why nothing the application's privacy policy, security policy, or terms cover changes.

On a record under the convergence rules (`rules: 2` or above), each acceptance criterion carries `tier: must` or `tier: should`. Must criteria are what the change promises: only they can block review or judgment, their number is capped by the declared risk (`must_criteria_cap` in `.ai/workflow.yml`), and the specification does not seal until a person records the exact list under `human_decisions` (boundary `must criteria approval`). Put a criterion in must when missing it would be a real failure of the change's purpose, security or data; everything else is should. A check only a person can make (open a published page, look at a render on a hosting site) is not a criterion: list it under `person_checks`, and `change release` carries it as a release item. Hold each criterion to the case it names. Do not write a catch-all rule that makes every statement in the code or the documentation binding ("any statement the code does not support is a defect"): it cannot be met, and it turns each later finding into a new round. Write bounded claims: what is checked, under which conditions, and the known gaps.
